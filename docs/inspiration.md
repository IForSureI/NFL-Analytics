# Inspiration

This portfolio started from two YouTube creators who make football analysis that people actually watch. I read the transcripts of 40 of their videos (21 MacKelvie, 19 Kollmann) and catalogued every title on both channels (52 long-form MacKelvie videos, 386 Kollmann videos) to learn how they pick questions, what data they trust, and how they show it. The one-line summaries below are my paraphrases.

## What I took from Michael MacKelvie

- **Start from a belief people repeat** ("flags even out", "defense wins championships") and test it.
- **Expected vs actual** as the core tool: EPA, win probability added, results minus expectation (luck).
- **Stability as the test of skill:** if a number does not repeat year to year or week to week, treat it as luck.
- **Simple filters as controls:** "normal football" situations instead of a black-box model.
- **Show one number, explain it in plain words,** and admit small samples.

## What I took from Brett Kollmann

- **The answer lives in the situation table,** not the season average (down, distance, field position).
- **Judge the decision apart from the result** (4th downs, two-point tries).
- **Numbers give the scale, film gives the mechanism.** Every chart here is built so a film clip could sit next to it.
- **End on a roster or contract decision,** because that is what a front office pays for.

## Videos read: Michael MacKelvie ([channel](https://www.youtube.com/@michaelmackelvie))

- [Does Defense Win Championships...?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=exOWLuFCkwg): Offense, especially passing, is more stable and predictive week to week than defense; defense is noisier, largely because its most valuable plays (turnovers, fumble recoveries) are close to random.
- [Investigating the Accuracy of College Football Recruiting Rankings](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVXWCnfLnQM): Star ratings predict draft status well and team titles strongly, but predict NFL stardom unevenly by position: accurate for "tangible" positions (RB, WR, DL), poor for intangible-heavy ones (QB, OL, LB, CB).
- [Momentum Exists - A Hot Hand Analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CR5vT44ZMK8): The "hot hand fallacy" was itself a fallacy.
- [Moneyball Was Supposed to Kill the Human Eye](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1gOX7a1kUNQ): Analytics did not kill scouting; MLB employs more scouts than ever.
- [NFL Draft Grades Are Wrong](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WuNkJb1kWbY): Draft grades built on "team need" and "reaches vs steals" miss the point.
- [NFL Teams Know How To Draft - Why Don't They?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Kdb2b0em-I): Every front office knows the research (early picks are overvalued; trade down and to the future), but GM incentives (short tenure, win-now pressure) make the "optimal" patient strategy a firing offense.
- [Passing is better...so, why run?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pPXZlK9puqw): Passing beats running on average, yet a rational offense must still run often because of game theory (a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium).
- [The Best Team is Not the Best Team](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zMMPfBydTeI): Talent is the single biggest driver of team success, but teams are not the sum of their parts.
- [The Clutch GOAT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qjjW1l9KjXQ): Defined by win-probability leverage ("clutch squared"), clutch reputations mostly reflect small samples and narrative.
- [The Most Important Man in Football is...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yr2CzT2mejg): The general manager, not the QB or coach, may be the most important figure.
- [The Safest Pick is the Most Dangerous in the NFL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H8rolEibsPg): "Safe" first-round picks at non-premium positions (TE, S, LB, IOL, RB) are the riskiest relative to return.
- [The best football player does not play...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bs6dAm72FPM): Elite play callers (McVay, Macdonald, Shanahan, Ben Johnson) win by being predictable pre-snap and unpredictable post-snap, pushing the decision tree later in the play.
- [The death of modern football is occurring...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wh9klmWAm5s): Defenses' shift to two-high pre-snap shells killed explosive passing, so offenses are moving back under center, re-adding fullbacks and paying explosive running backs.
- [This is 97% of winning in football - nobody knows what it is](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zcqxf7_TsI8): Players have the ball ~3% of the time; winning is about creating and occupying valuable space.
- [We Measured "Luck" in the NFL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zfin10hS0YM): Football has few possessions and single-elimination playoffs, so chance events swing outcomes and do not "even out." Using the NFL's four luck categories, luck strongly predicts playoff wins and can add multiple regular-season wins.
- [We looked at 92,255 flags - NFL refs are biased](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LH3Ck59I8V0): Penalties do not "even out." Subjective flags are random in frequency, biased by context (time, home crowd, sideline), and a few well-timed big flags swing playoff berths.
- [Why Teams Come Back - The Rubber Band Effect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8By4b6cTVFU): Trailing teams score far more late in games and leading teams play "like losers." Prevent-style soft coverage (drop eight, deep safeties) does not stop late drives; defenses over-fear the deep ball and get carved by 7-10 play drives, while offenses play too conservatively earlier.
- [Why We Still Don't Know What a "Good" Quarterback Is](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ZenuMSB814): Quarterback production swings a lot with scheme and supporting cast (Mahomes 2023-25, Goff, Darnold, Baker), so nobody can reliably define "good" outside the elite few.
- [Why We Still Don't Really Know Who's Good at Football - Entanglement Analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PUJY1xUQSJA): Football performance is "entangled": a player's numbers depend on teammates, scheme and coaches.
- [You Will Never Look at College Football the Same](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LhJlEId04oA): College football's product is regional community and rivalry, not elite talent.
- [You Will Never Look at the NFL Draft the Same](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1-0xWcSYlh4): NFL teams massively overvalue early and present-year picks.

## Videos read: Brett Kollmann ([channel](https://www.youtube.com/@BrettKollmann))

- ["Two high safeties are ruining football"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fGRkKmXGZr8): The viral take is wrong: the league plays more single-high Cover 3 than a decade ago.
- [Are analytics ruining football?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOnQFk5qy-4): (Week 15, 2021.) Critics of aggressive fourth-down and two-point decisions (Staley vs KC, Harbaugh vs GB) confuse process with execution.
- [Does "establishing the run" even work?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-W6orkJ_-Qg): Defenses don't react to a team's yards-per-carry; they react to down-and-distance run/pass tendencies.
- [How Accountants Broke the NFL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UR9aAOiaVVE): Headline AAV is a "vanity number." Modern mega-contracts use prorated signing bonuses, stacked rolling option bonuses and void years to widen the "surface area" of cap hits.
- [How to Watch NFL Game Film (100,000 Subscriber Special)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a6ZL9R4IKMg): (2018.) His film process, shown on one unused play (Micah Hyde's INT vs Atlanta, Week 4 2017): pause pre-snap and diagnose as the QB would, form expectations of coverage and its weak spots, then rewatch repeatedly, focusing on surrounding defenders' body language when the result contradicts the design.
- [Kick Returners are in Absolute Hell.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELjLAp4eyic): (Early 2025.) Two quiet rule changes (touchback moved to the 35; teams may pre-prepare K-balls) made elite kickers quasi-defensive weapons.
- [Kickers are taking over the NFL.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UufAB2UcVMw): Under the 2024 dynamic kickoff, returns behave like run plays (trap blocks, lead blockers), so return rates and big returns rise.
- [Not all sacks are created equal.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o6q1dd0mI8Q): Good third-down defenses are really good early-down defenses: third-down stop rate is driven by average distance to go, and a second-down sack (which usually creates 3rd-and-11+) can be worth more in lost EPA than many third-down sacks.
- [QB sneaks are up 319% and it's terrible.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7oO8bjbtPqA): The tush push isn't unfair or dangerous; the real reason it may get banned is that it's boring television.
- [Should Running Backs go in the Top 10?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8ymN2_OBGuE): "Your offensive line sets your floor and your running back sets your ceiling." An elite back (Barkley, Jeanty) only pays off at a top-10 price if the line already creates yards before contact.
- [The Anatomy of a Blowout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZN_hPJxI_s8): Green Bay's 48-32 Wild Card rout of Dallas (2023 season) was decided by roughly four plays, each one the result of a scouted tendency: Dallas's field-side return routes, its third-down red-zone Cover 0 habit, and CeeDee Lamb's slant volume.
- [The Dolphins ran the same blitz 35 times in one game.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lxKL678g5WE): (2021 TNF, MIA 22-10 BAL.) Brian Flores's Dolphins showed Cover 0 pre-snap on 40 snaps and ran a true zero pressure ~34-35 times.
- [The NFL hired me](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3GMdkEp6cpE): (Summer 2026; short announcement, ~5 min.) Career-arc update: from NFL Network intern and production assistant (RedZone graphics, GameDay Morning, Total Access) to full-time YouTuber (left the day before Super Bowl LI), and now back with NFL Media as on-camera talent on a new NFL Pro channel show with Trevor Sikkema, with access to league tools and resources.
- [The Running Back Problem.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PLGJLH4xQcA): (Summer 2023.) Veteran RBs are underpaid relative to workload and impact, but teams act rationally: cheap rookies deliver most of an elite back's production at a fraction of the cost, and by free agency an RB's prime is ending.
- [The Strangest First Round Pick Ever](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o41tGtv4DrE): (2022 draft.) Cole Strange (FCS Chattanooga, Patriots pick 29) was not a reach: he is an elite athlete, a better technician than credited, and his first step lets him make reach blocks most guards can't, which unlocks wide-zone runs to the front side.
- [The game that made playing defense illegal.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vqE_fM18K6Y): In the 2003 AFC Championship (Colts at Patriots), Peyton Manning's four interceptions came mostly from his own mistakes and great DB play (three by Ty Law), not illegal contact.
- [What broke the Quarterback pipeline?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ABoLgCdTgZc): QB "late bloomers" are mostly a structural problem: college hash widths and a looser ineligible-downfield rule reward screens, RPOs and shotgun quick game, so prospects arrive never having run under-center play action, the skill that powers NFL run games and explosive passing.
- [When 28 Pro Bowlers met in the AFC Championship](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9p3hdAyjhQQ): A nostalgic, play-by-play film retrospective of the 2008 AFC Championship (Steelers 23, Ravens 14): two defense-first heavyweights built to win with "body blows." Key moments are explained through scheme: Rex Ryan's weak-side four-man stunts, Polamalu reading the fake-sneak tell, a Ray Lewis bust in "3 Buzz Mable" on 2nd-and-24, and Polamalu's pick-six enabled by Dick LeBeau's two-deep fire zone "Sting."
- [Why only 30% of first round quarterbacks succeed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BhQRXLhYRO4): (Early 2022.) A self-critical retrospective on his own QB evaluation misses (Hackenberg over Prescott, Kizer over Mahomes/Watson, Rosen/Darnold over Allen/Jackson, Tua/Hurts over Herbert).
